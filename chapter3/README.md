# Chapter 3: The Principle of Relativity

## 1. A New Question

In Chapter 1, we discussed the law of conservation of momentum.

It tells us that change in the universe does not occur without rules. A system can collide, split apart, and reassemble, but there are invariant constraints throughout the process of change. A system does not lose its own continuity because of change. This provides the first layer of physical foundation for Zero-Bug Degree in GULP.

In Chapter 2, we discussed the principle of the invariance of the speed of light.

It tells us that change in the universe does not propagate instantaneously. All information, energy, and influence have a maximum speed of propagation—the speed of light. This imposes a limit on the causal structure of the universe.

But a new question arises. If every observer in the universe is in a different state of motion, then: do different observers see the same universe?

For example: Xiaoming stands on Earth. Xiaohong sits in a spaceship flying at high speed. They observe the same event at the same time. Will the time, distance, and speed that Xiaoming sees be exactly the same as the time, distance, and speed that Xiaohong sees? If they are not the same, then what is the real universe? Does the universe have an absolutely correct observation angle?

This question leads us to the principle of relativity.

---

## 2. Newton's Absolute Universe

Before Newtonian mechanics was established, people believed that space was a fixed grand stage, and time was a uniformly flowing river. No matter where anyone stood, no matter how anyone moved, space was the same space, and time was the same time. This is Newton's absolute view of space and time.

In this world, different observers only need to convert speeds. For example: a high-speed train moves forward at 300 kilometers per hour. A person on the train throws a ball forward at 20 kilometers per hour. Then a person on the ground sees the ball's speed as 300 + 20 = 320 kilometers per hour. This is classical velocity addition. In Newton's universe, speed can increase without limit, time never changes, and space never changes. Everything is very intuitive.

---

## 3. The Contradiction Brought by Light

But light shattered this simple world.

According to classical velocity addition: if a spaceship moves at near light speed, and a person on the spaceship turns on a flashlight, an observer on the ground should see the speed of light equal to the spaceship's speed plus the speed of light—in other words, light should exceed the speed of light. But experiments tell us: no. No matter how the spaceship moves, no matter how the observer moves, the measured speed of light is always about three hundred thousand kilometers per second.

In 1887, Michelson and Morley used an interferometer to repeatedly measure the effect of the Earth's motion on the speed of light. The result was zero. More than one hundred and thirty years have passed, and the conclusion of this experiment remains unchanged to this day.

This creates a huge contradiction: if space and time are completely fixed, then the invariance of the speed of light cannot hold. But experiments tell us that the speed of light must remain invariant. Then something must have changed.

---

## 4. What Must the Universe Change?

The formula for the speed of light is simple: speed equals distance divided by time. If the speed must remain invariant, then distance and time must coordinate with each other.

Suppose different observers measure the same beam of light. One observer believes the light traveled a distance s and took time t. Another observer believes the light traveled a distance s' and took time t'. They must satisfy s/t = c and s'/t' = c, otherwise the speed of light would change. This means that between different observers, distance and time cannot be completely independent. Space and time must be linked together. They are not two completely separate things, but rather form a unified whole: spacetime.

---

## 5. The Principle of Relativity

Einstein further proposed that the laws of physics are the same in all inertial reference frames.

What is an inertial reference frame? Simply put, it is an observer who is not subject to obvious external forces, maintaining uniform motion or at rest. For example: a car parked on the ground, a high-speed train moving in a straight line at constant speed, a spaceship flying smoothly. In these reference frames, the laws of physics do not change because the observer is different. No observer has a special status. The Earth is not the center of the universe. The Sun is not the center of the universe. No observer can say, "My time is the real time," or "My space is the real space." The universe has no absolute observer.

---

## 6. A Simple Experiment

Imagine a sealed spaceship flying at constant speed through space. The people inside perform all kinds of experiments: throwing a ball, measuring the speed of light, observing the motion of objects. Without windows, they cannot know whether they are at rest or in uniform motion, because all the laws of physics inside the spaceship are the same.

If the spaceship accelerates, the situation is different, because acceleration means being subject to change. But uniform motion itself produces no special sensation. This shows that motion itself is not absolute; only motion relative to other objects has meaning.

---

## 7. The Principle of Relativity and GULP

The principle of relativity has an important implication for GULP: the evolution of the universe does not depend on any single special observation angle.

A truly stable law must satisfy the following: observers can be different, descriptions can be different, but the underlying logic must remain consistent. This is consistent with the idea of Zero-Bug Degree. The external manifestations of a system can change, but the rules that maintain the system's operation cannot be arbitrarily altered. Just like in a sports match, different spectators sit in different positions and see different angles, but the rules of the game do not change.

---

## 8. From the Principle of Relativity to the Lorentz Transformation

At this point, we have arrived at two important conditions. First, all inertial observers must see the same laws of physics. Second, all inertial observers must measure the same speed of light.

But these two conditions, when combined, produce a new question: if different observers measure different times and distances, then how exactly do time and space convert between them?

Newton's conversion method can no longer satisfy the invariance of the speed of light. The universe requires a new rule for transforming space and time. This rule is the Lorentz transformation.
