# Part I: The Origin of the Universe

## Chapter 1: The First Law of the Universe — Conservation of Momentum

---

### 1. An Honest Start to the Question of Cosmic Origins

Where did the universe come from? In the vast cosmos, where do we even begin?

To be honest, we don't know.

What is a singularity? We don't know. What came before the singularity? We don't know. How was inflation triggered? We don't know. How did inflation stop? We don't know. Does quark-gluon plasma actually exist? We don't know. Can we trace an evolutionary curve from the singularity to the nebula? We cannot. There is too much we don't know about the origin of the universe. Every unknown is a hole we fall into and cannot climb out of.

The Big Bang theory attempts to answer this question. It offers a complete narrative — the universe began from a singularity, went through inflation, primordial nucleosynthesis, and photon decoupling, eventually forming galaxies and stars. This story has been written into textbooks, accepted by countless people, and treated as the standard model.

### 2. GULP's Three Criticisms of the Big Bang Theory

But GULP doesn't accept this account. It raises three specific criticisms.

The first: missing evolutionary dynamics. The Big Bang theory describes the process of cosmic expansion, yet it never explains where the driving force for that expansion came from. Why did inflation begin? Why did it end? Where is the engine?

The second: a broken evolutionary curve. From the singularity to the nebula, too many segments lack continuous observational data. The timeline is riddled with gaps.

The third: virtual nodes treated as confirmed nodes. Quarks have never been directly observed, and the early universe's quark-gluon plasma is an even more speculative state. Taking a conjecture as fact and then building an even earlier model of the universe on top of that fact is proving a hypothesis with another hypothesis.

We have to admit that these criticisms point to real weaknesses in the Big Bang theory. Physical laws break down at the singularity. There is no direct evidence for the inflation mechanism. The evolutionary curve from the singularity to the nebula is full of fractures. The available chain of evidence is simply too sparse for us to piece that period of history together. Perhaps one day, new observational data will fill these gaps. But until then, these criticisms stand.

### 3. Solving the One Who Raised the Problem — GULP

The question remains, and for now it cannot be answered. So let us turn to the one who raised the problem: GULP. Does GULP itself have a problem? Its sole core principle is Zero-Bug Degree. What physical basis does it have?

The meaning of Zero-Bug Degree is simple: can a system, amid change, maintain its own structure and consistency? If it can, it persists. If it cannot, it disintegrates. It is not about being flawless, nor about being static. It refers to dynamic stability — the ability of a system under constant disturbance to maintain its own continuity through internal regulation.

So, what law in physics fits this description? What law guarantees that a system, amid change, will not spontaneously collapse? The first correspondence of Zero-Bug Degree in the physical world is the law of conservation of momentum.

### 4. Conservation of Momentum Validates GULP

The law of conservation of momentum is stated as follows: when a system is subject to no external force, or the sum of external forces is zero, the total momentum of the system remains constant.

Its formula is:

p = mv

p is momentum, m is mass, and v is velocity. Momentum is a vector, possessing both magnitude and direction. For a closed system, the total momentum before a collision equals the total momentum after the collision:

m₁v₁ + m₂v₂ = m₁v₁' + m₂v₂'

No matter what happens inside the system — collisions, explosions, reorganizations — the total momentum is always a fixed value. The system can change in countless ways, but the changes are constrained within an invariant framework. This is the most fundamental physical manifestation of dynamic stability.

This law was not conjured out of thin air. Descartes first proposed the concept of conservation of quantity of motion. Huygens discovered that momentum must account for direction. Newton's third law — for every action there is an equal and opposite reaction — directly implies conservation of momentum. When two objects collide, A exerts a force on B, and B exerts an equal and opposite force on A for the same duration. The momentum A loses is exactly the momentum B gains. The total momentum does not change.

But the most fundamental proof comes from Noether's theorem. In 1918, Emmy Noether proved that every continuous symmetry corresponds to a conservation law. Spatial translational symmetry — the fact that the laws of physics are the same everywhere — corresponds to the conservation of momentum. This means that conservation of momentum is not an empirical regularity stumbled upon in experiments, but a mathematical necessity of the structure of spacetime itself. As long as space is symmetric, momentum must be conserved. This is not a law that can be overturned. It is a law that cannot be violated.

Noether's theorem requires the symmetry to be continuous. Spatial translation can take arbitrarily small values. The partial derivatives of the Lagrangian with respect to coordinates must exist. The Euler-Lagrange equations must be solvable. Only then can the derivation of the conserved quantity be completed. Continuity, integrability, and differentiability — these three together form the mathematical foundation of Noether's theorem.

This also proves something of crucial importance: the evolutionary curve must be continuous. When a system evolves from one state to the next, there is no break in between. If the evolutionary curve had a jump or a break, it would mean the system violated the equations of dynamics at some moment — and those equations are derived from continuous symmetry. Conservation of momentum forces the evolutionary curve to be continuous. This is not GULP's demand. It is the demand of spacetime structure itself.

Conversely, if the evolutionary curve did have a jump — if the system leaped directly from t₁ to t₂, skipping the states in between — then how did p₁ become p₂? In the missing time, what force acted for how long, producing how much impulse? It would all be a blank. The change in momentum would have no cause, and conservation of momentum would be violated. A jump is not "unknown." It is "impossible."

At this point, the law of conservation of momentum has simultaneously validated two of GULP's core concepts: the continuity of the evolutionary curve, and Zero-Bug Degree.

The evolutionary curve is continuous because conservation of momentum forbids breaks in the timeline. Zero-Bug Degree holds because conservation of momentum provides a skeleton for the system that cannot be shaken apart no matter what happens internally — the total momentum is always a fixed value, and the system, amid change, will not spontaneously collapse. Zero-Bug Degree is not a philosophical fantasy. It has found a physical entity in the conservation of momentum. The continuity of the evolutionary curve is not a methodological preference. It has found mathematical compulsion in the conservation of momentum. One law, striking two concepts at once.

Moreover, conservation of momentum directly demands the existence of evolutionary dynamics. The current momentum of a system is a fixed value. If the momentum changes in the next moment, there is only one possibility: an external force has intervened. Force multiplied by the duration of its action is impulse. Impulse changes momentum. A change in momentum must have a source, and that source is force. How much did it change? That tells you the magnitude and direction of the force. Where is the engine? Why did it accelerate? Why did it decelerate? Every step must have an answer. There is no causeless change of velocity. There is no engine-less evolution.

GULP's Zero-Bug Degree has found its physical entity in the conservation of momentum. And the question of evolutionary dynamics — that we leave to the next chapter, on the principle of the invariance of the speed of light.
