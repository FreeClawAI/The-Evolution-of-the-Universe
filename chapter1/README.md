### 1. An Honest Start to the Question of Cosmic Origins

Where did the universe come from? In the vast cosmos, where do we even begin?

The Big Bang theory attempts to answer these questions. It offers a complete picture—beginning from an extremely hot and dense early state, expanding through inflation, primordial nucleosynthesis, and photon decoupling, eventually forming galaxies and stars. This theory has been written into textbooks, supported by countless observations, and treated as the standard model.

We have to admit that the Big Bang theory does leave some unresolved problems. Physical laws break down at the singularity. There is no direct evidence for the inflation mechanism. The evolutionary curve from the singularity to the nebula still contains many observational gaps. The available chain of evidence is not yet sufficient for us to piece that period of history together completely. Perhaps one day, new observational data will fill these gaps.

The questions remain, and for now they cannot be fully resolved. So let us turn to GULP—the framework that raised these questions—and see whether it stands on solid ground. GULP's core concept is Zero-Bug Degree. What physical basis does it have?

### 2. Zero-Bug Degree

The meaning of Zero-Bug Degree is simple: can a system, amid change, maintain its own structure and consistency? If it can, it persists. If it cannot, it disintegrates. It is not about being flawless, nor about being static and unchanging. It refers to dynamic stability—the ability of a system, under constant disturbance, to maintain its own continuity through internal regulation.

So, what law in physics fits this description? What law guarantees that a system, amid change, will not spontaneously collapse?

The first correspondence of Zero-Bug Degree in the physical world is the law of conservation of momentum.

---

### 3. The Law of Conservation of Momentum

The law of conservation of momentum is stated as follows: when a system is subject to no external force, or the sum of external forces is zero, the total momentum of the system remains constant.

Its formula is simple:

p = mv

p is momentum, m is mass, and v is velocity. Momentum is a vector, possessing both magnitude and direction. For a closed system, the total momentum before a collision equals the total momentum after the collision:

m₁v₁ + m₂v₂ = m₁v₁' + m₂v₂'

No matter what happens inside the system—collisions, explosions, reorganizations—the total momentum is always a fixed value. The system can change in countless ways, but the changes are constrained within an invariant framework. This is the most fundamental physical manifestation of dynamic stability.

This law was not conjured out of thin air. Descartes first proposed the concept of conservation of quantity of motion. Huygens discovered that momentum must account for direction. Newton's third law—for every action there is an equal and opposite reaction—directly implies conservation of momentum. When two objects collide, A exerts a force on B, and B exerts an equal and opposite force on A for the same duration. The momentum A loses is exactly the momentum B gains. The total momentum does not change.

---

### 4. Noether's Theorem

A deeper understanding of momentum conservation comes from Noether's theorem. In 1918, Emmy Noether proved a profound mathematical theorem: every continuous symmetry corresponds to a conservation law.

Simply put, if the universe maintains some hidden invariance before and after a certain kind of change, a corresponding conserved quantity will emerge. Spatial translational symmetry—the fact that the laws of physics are the same here and there—corresponds to the conservation of momentum. Time translational symmetry—the fact that the laws of physics are the same today and tomorrow—corresponds to the conservation of energy.

This means that conservation of momentum is not an empirical regularity stumbled upon by chance in experiments, but a logical necessity of spacetime symmetry. Within our current physical framework, as long as spatial translational symmetry holds, conservation of momentum is an inevitable result.

Noether's theorem requires the symmetry to be continuous. Spatial translation can take arbitrarily small values. The partial derivatives of the Lagrangian with respect to coordinates must exist. The Euler-Lagrange equations must be solvable. Only then can the derivation of the conserved quantity be completed. Continuity, integrability, and differentiability—these three together form the mathematical foundation of Noether's theorem.

### 5. Conservation of Momentum and the Evolutionary Curve

This also provides a physical constraint for the continuity of the evolutionary curve.

Within the framework of continuous spacetime and continuous dynamics, conservation of momentum requires that changes in a system's state must be completed through continuous processes. When a system evolves from one state to the next, there can be no causeless jumps in between.

If the evolutionary curve did have a jump—if the system leaped directly from t₁ to t₂, skipping the states in between—then how did p₁ become p₂? In the missing time, what interaction produced how much impulse? It would all be a blank. For continuous dynamical processes at classical scales, states cannot jump without cause.

### 6. Conservation of Momentum and Zero-Bug Degree

At this point, the law of conservation of momentum has provided solid physical support for GULP's Zero-Bug Degree.

Zero-Bug Degree requires that a system not collapse amid change. Conservation of momentum happens to provide the system with a skeleton that cannot be shaken apart no matter what happens internally—the total momentum is always a fixed value. Zero-Bug Degree is not a philosophical fantasy. It has found a physical correspondence in the conservation of momentum.

At the same time, conservation of momentum also validates the continuity of the evolutionary curve—within the framework of continuous spacetime and continuous dynamics, changes of state must be completed through continuous processes. One law, simultaneously providing a physical foundation for two of GULP's core inquiries.

### 7. Evolutionary Dynamics: An Unresolved Question

But one question remains unanswered.

Conservation of momentum tells us that the momentum of a system remains unchanged only when no external force acts upon it. Conversely, if the momentum changes, external factors must have intervened. The amount of change allows us to trace back the magnitude and direction of the acting force. Where is the engine? Why accelerate? Why decelerate? Every step must have an answer.

This is evolutionary dynamics. What it asks is not "what remains unchanged," but "what drives the change."

Returning to GULP's critique of the Big Bang theory. The Big Bang theory describes the evolutionary history of the universe from the earliest moments to today, but it has not fully answered: why did inflation begin? Why did inflation end? Who drives the acceleration and deceleration of spatial expansion? Evolutionary dynamics demands that every step of change have a traceable cause, and at present, our understanding of these driving forces still contains gaps.

Conservation of momentum provides the starting point for evolutionary dynamics—change must have a source. But it does not tell us what that source is, or how it propagates. When a local event occurs, how does it affect other parts of the system? Does it arrive instantaneously, or does it propagate at a finite speed?

The answers to these questions do not lie within the conservation of momentum. They require another law.
