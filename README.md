# Grand Unified Logic Chain: The Evolution of the Universe

## Preface

The evolution of the universe. We begin with the origin of the universe.

There are several mainstream versions. The main versions regarding the origin of the universe are roughly as follows:

**The Big Bang Theory.** The universe began expanding from an extremely hot and dense initial state, went through inflation, primordial nucleosynthesis, and photon decoupling, eventually forming galaxies and stars. This is the mainstream model with the most observational evidence, but unresolved problems remain regarding singularity physics and the inflation mechanism.

**The Cyclic Universe Model.** The universe undergoes infinite cycles of expansion and contraction, with each Big Bang being a bounce rather than an absolute beginning. This avoids the singularity problem but lacks direct observational evidence.

**Quantum Gravity Models.** In the framework of quantum gravity, the universe has no boundary, and time manifests as a spatial dimension in the early stage. The singularity is eliminated by quantum effects, and the universe is self-contained, requiring no starting point.

**The Multiverse Model.** Inflation not only produced our observable universe, but also produced countless disconnected universes on a larger scale. Our universe is just one bubble among them.

**String Theory / Brane World Models.** The universe originated from the collision of two branes in higher-dimensional space, with the Big Bang being the result of this collision. These models attempt to unify gravity with quantum mechanics but currently cannot be experimentally verified.

---

Each of these models has its own claims and its own unresolved issues. They are all trying to answer the same question: how did the universe begin?

But perhaps we should first ask a different question: can the evolution of the universe be reconstructed?

To reconstruct a segment of evolutionary history, three questions must be asked simultaneously. First, what drives the change? This is evolutionary dynamics. Second, are the logical connections between changes complete? This is evolutionary continuity. Third, how does the system remain stable through change without collapsing? This is systemic stability—what we call Zero-Bug Degree. If any one of these three is missing, reconstruction cannot be completed.

Examining existing models of cosmic origin through these three questions reveals some common difficulties.

Evolutionary dynamics asks: who stepped on the accelerator? The Big Bang theory describes the process of cosmic expansion, but why inflation started and why it stopped remain open questions. Modern inflation theory proposes scalar field dynamics, but the deeper origins of these mechanisms—what the inflaton field is, why it exists, why it chose that particular potential—remain unknown. This is not a case of having no answers; it is a case of not yet having traced the answers to their deepest level.

Evolutionary continuity asks: do the footprints connect? From the earliest moments to today, the universe's evolutionary history contains too many segments that lack direct observational data. Quarks cannot be individually captured and directly observed in the way electrons and protons can; they are theoretical entities established through high-energy scattering experiments, strong interaction theory, and a wealth of independent experimental data. In scientific methodology, this kind of indirect verification is entirely legitimate. But when quark-gluon plasma is used as a precondition for primordial nucleosynthesis, between quarks and the hydrogen-helium ratio lie hadronization, neutrino decoupling, and matter-antimatter annihilation—none of these links are currently directly observable. This segment of the evolutionary curve is built on inference rather than observation. This is not a mistake; it is a lack of information. If a theory is built upon nodes that have not yet been fully verified, the uncertainty of those nodes propagates into all subsequent deductions.

Zero-Bug Degree asks: why doesn't the system collapse amid change? The universe expanded from an extremely hot and dense state to today, with structures emerging layer upon layer, yet it never collapsed at any stage. The speed of spatial expansion changed three times—extremely fast during inflation, suddenly slowing after it ended, and accelerating again billions of years later. Each of these speed changes should have a dynamical cause. We currently have mathematical models that describe these changes, but the deep driving forces behind the speed changes remain to be explored.

None of this is a denial of the Big Bang theory. The Big Bang theory is one of the bravest theoretical reconstructions in the history of science. A group of physicists, armed only with the temperature fluctuations of the cosmic microwave background, the hydrogen-helium ratio in the universe, and the recession speeds of distant galaxies—just these few fragments—pieced together a history of the universe from minutes after creation all the way to today. This imagination and courage deserve praise no matter the era.

But acknowledging a theory's greatness does not mean we stop asking the questions it has not yet answered. Can evolutionary dynamics be traced further? Can the breaks in the evolutionary curve be filled by new observations? Does Zero-Bug Degree have a deeper physical foundation at the cosmic scale? These questions are not meant to overturn the Big Bang theory; they are meant to go where it has not yet gone.

So where do we begin our journey through the evolution of the universe?

We begin where we can truly see. We begin with observational facts that require no assumptions and no guesses—things we can point to in the sky and say, "It is there." We will use these nodes to build a tree of cosmic evolution. Every leaf is a verified observational fact. Every branch is a causal connection that has passed the Zero-Bug Degree test. We do not know where the crown of this tree will ultimately reach, but every step will be planted on solid logical nodes.

This is not a journey whose destination is already known. We do not hold a complete map of the universe.

So let us begin this journey in search of the logical chain of cosmic evolution.
