# Grand Unified Logic Chain: The Evolution of the Universe

## Preface

We begin our exploration of the universe's evolution at the very beginning.

There are several mainstream versions of cosmic origin. Here are the most prominent ones:

**1. The Big Bang Theory (Standard Cosmological Model)**

The universe began expanding from an extremely hot and dense initial state, undergoing inflation, primordial nucleosynthesis, and photon decoupling, eventually forming galaxies and stars. This is the mainstream model with the most observational evidence, yet unresolved questions remain regarding singularity physics and the inflation mechanism.

**2. The Cyclic Universe Model**

The universe undergoes infinite cycles of expansion and contraction, with each Big Bang being a bounce rather than an absolute beginning. This avoids the singularity problem but lacks direct observational evidence.

**3. Quantum Gravity Models (e.g., Hawking-Hartle No-Boundary Proposal)**

Within the framework of quantum gravity, the universe has no boundary, and time manifests as a spatial dimension in the early universe. The singularity is eliminated by quantum effects. The universe is self-contained and requires no "beginning."

**4. The Multiverse Model (Inflationary Multiverse)**

Inflation not only produced our observable universe but also generated countless disconnected universes on a larger scale. Our universe is just one bubble among many.

**5. String Theory / Brane World Model**

The universe originated from the collision of two branes in higher-dimensional space, with the Big Bang being the result of this collision. This model attempts to unify gravity with quantum mechanics but currently lacks experimental verification.

---

Each of these models has its own narrative, and each has its own unanswered questions. We will analyze them using the Grand Unified Logic Chain (GULP).

GULP does not ask how the universe began. It asks only one question: Can the evolution of the universe be reconstructed?

To reconstruct any evolutionary process, three conditions must be satisfied simultaneously: Zero-Bug Degree, evolutionary dynamics, and the evolutionary curve. If any one of these is missing, reconstruction is impossible.

Let us begin with evolutionary dynamics. In plain terms, this asks: What pressed the accelerator?

The Big Bang Theory describes in detail the processes of cosmic expansion—inflation, primordial nucleosynthesis, photon decoupling—but it never explains why these processes occurred. What was the engine of inflation? Where did the energy come from? What triggered inflation to start, and what caused it to stop? The Big Bang Theory offers only labels—inflaton, dark energy, cosmological constant—but labels are not engines. It tells us the story of expansion without telling us where the first driving force came from. A universe without an engine, in GULP's assessment, cannot be reconstructed.

Now let us consider the evolutionary curve. In plain terms, this asks: Do the footprints connect?

The evolutionary curve requires that the universe's changes over time maintain continuity on the timeline, with no gaps in between. But the Big Bang Theory's evolutionary curve is riddled with fracture zones. The most fatal crack is called the quark.

Quarks have never been directly observed. They remain, to this day, a virtual node—physicists pieced together scattered experimental data to construct a logic chain, and from this chain the existence of quarks was inferred. In GULP, this is called "virtual construction," and it is legitimate in itself. But virtual construction has one iron rule: an unverified virtual node cannot serve as a deterministic foundation for further deduction. The Big Bang Theory violates this rule. It treats quarks as real building blocks, using them to construct models of the even earlier universe, to explain quark-gluon plasma, to set preconditions for primordial nucleosynthesis. One assumption is built upon another. The front half of the evolutionary curve is suspended on an unverified foundation. If the quark model is ever revised, the entire curve breaks in the middle.

And there is more. Even if we accept quarks, the evolutionary curve faces a more intuitive challenge: speed.

If the universe originated from the explosion of a singularity, with particles hurtling outward in all directions, then in 13.8 billion years they would have to traverse a distance of 46 billion light-years. Their average speed would have to be more than three times the speed of light. No particle with mass can achieve this. Even if we grant that they traveled at nearly the speed of light, the kinetic energy of a single proton would be equivalent to a fully loaded truck slamming into a point the size of an atomic nucleus—and there would be countless such protons. Even more absurd is that these particles somehow slowed down. The universe we observe today has nebulae contracting gently under gravity and galaxies rotating at a leisurely pace. Where did that ferocious initial velocity go? What force applied the brakes?

Proponents of the Big Bang Theory would respond: it is not the particles flying through space, but space itself expanding. General relativity permits space to stretch faster than light. Particles are merely carried along by space. This explanation transfers the speed problem from particles to space, but the ledger is not erased. Why does space speed up and slow down? Who controls the switch that starts and stops inflation? Why did the universe begin accelerating again billions of years later? Cosmology has given names to these three speed changes, but names are not answers.

No engine. No continuous footprints. GULP cannot reconstruct the history from the singularity to the nebulae.

This is not to say the Big Bang Theory is wrong. On the contrary, it represents the most courageous theoretical reconstruction in the history of human science. A group of physicists, armed with nothing more than temperature fluctuations in the cosmic microwave background, the hydrogen-to-helium ratio in the universe, and the recession velocities of distant galaxies—just these few fragments—pieced together a cosmic history stretching from minutes after creation to the present day. This imagination and courage deserve admiration beyond measure.

But courage is not the same as correctness. Admiration cannot substitute for verification. We can applaud a theory's heroism, but we cannot overlook its logical fractures because of that applause. By GULP's standards, the Big Bang Theory steps over all three red lines: missing evolutionary dynamics, a broken evolutionary curve, and virtual nodes treated as deterministic foundations.

So where does our cosmic evolution begin?

It begins where we can truly see. It begins with logic nodes that require no assumptions, no guesses—nodes we can point to in the sky and say, "There it is." With these nodes, we will construct a tree of the Grand Unified Logic Chain. Every leaf is a verified observational fact. Every branch is a causal connection that has passed the Zero-Bug Degree test. We do not know where the crown of this tree will ultimately reach, but GULP will illuminate every step beneath our feet.

This is not a journey whose destination we already know. We hold no complete map of the universe.
