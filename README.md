# Grand Unified Logical Chain: The Evolution of the Universe

by 林佑(linyou)

## Preface

Regarding the origin of the universe, there are currently several mainstream versions:

**The Big Bang Theory** holds that the universe began expanding from an extremely hot and dense initial state, undergoing inflation, primordial nucleosynthesis, photon decoupling, and ultimately forming galaxies and stars. This is the mainstream model with the most observational evidence, but unresolved problems remain in singularity physics and the inflation mechanism.

**The Cyclic Universe Model** proposes that the universe undergoes infinite cycles of expansion and contraction, with each Big Bang being a rebound rather than an absolute beginning. This avoids the singularity problem but lacks direct observational evidence.

**Quantum Gravity Models** suggest that within the framework of quantum gravity, the universe has no boundary, time behaves as a spatial dimension in the early universe, the singularity is eliminated by quantum effects, and the universe is self-contained, requiring no starting point.

**The Multiverse Model** proposes that inflation not only produced our observable universe but also generated countless mutually disconnected universes on larger scales. Our universe is merely one bubble among many.

**String Theory Brane World Models** propose that the universe originated from the collision of two branes in higher-dimensional space, with the Big Bang being the result of this brane collision. This model attempts to unify gravity and quantum mechanics but currently cannot be experimentally verified.

---

Each of these models has its own narrative and its own unresolved questions. They are all attempting to answer the same question: How did the universe begin?

But perhaps we should first ask a different question: **Can the evolution of the universe be reconstructed?**

To reconstruct a process of evolution, three questions must be pursued simultaneously. First, what drives the change?—this is evolutionary dynamics. Second, is the logical connection between changes complete?—this is evolutionary continuity. Third, how does the system maintain stability through change without collapsing?—this is system stability, which we call Zero-Bug Degree. If any one of these three is missing, reconstruction is impossible.

Examining existing models of cosmic origins through these three questions reveals some common difficulties.

Evolutionary dynamics asks: Who stepped on the accelerator? The Big Bang Theory describes the process of cosmic expansion, but why inflation started and why it stopped remain open questions. Modern inflation theory proposes scalar field dynamical mechanisms, but the deeper sources—what the inflaton field is, why it exists, why that particular potential was chosen—remain unknown. This is not the absence of an answer; it is that the answer has not yet been pursued to its root.

Evolutionary continuity asks: Along this journey, do the footprints connect? The evolutionary history from the very early universe to today contains too many segments that lack direct observational data. Quarks, unlike electrons and protons, cannot be isolated and detected as free particles; their existence is primarily confirmed through the theory of strong interactions and indirect evidence from high-energy experiments. Within scientific methodology, such indirect verification is entirely legitimate. But when quark-gluon plasma is used as a precondition for primordial nucleosynthesis, between quarks and the hydrogen-helium ratio lie hadronization, neutrino decoupling, and matter-antimatter annihilation—segments that currently cannot be directly observed. This stretch of the evolutionary curve is built on inference, not observation. This is not an error; it is insufficient information. If a theory is built upon nodes that have not yet been fully verified, the uncertainty of these nodes propagates into subsequent deductions.

Zero-Bug Degree asks: Why does the system not collapse amid change? The universe expanded from an extremely hot and dense state to what it is today, with structures emerging layer upon layer, yet it never disintegrated at any stage. The expansion history of the universe has passed through different dominant phases, from early inflation, through radiation-dominated and matter-dominated eras, to the present-day accelerated expansion driven by dark energy. Behind each shift in dominance there should be a dynamical cause. We currently have mathematical models describing these changes, but the deep driving forces behind the shifts still await exploration.

These are not denials of the Big Bang Theory. The Big Bang Theory is one of the most courageous theoretical reconstructions in the history of human science. A group of physicists, armed with nothing more than a few fragments—temperature fluctuations in the cosmic microwave background, the hydrogen-helium ratio in the universe, the recession velocities of distant galaxies—pieced together a cosmic history stretching from minutes after creation to the present day. This imagination and courage cannot be praised enough.

But acknowledging the greatness of a theory does not mean ceasing to question the problems it has yet to answer. Can evolutionary dynamics be pursued further? Can the gaps in the evolutionary curve be filled by new observations? Does Zero-Bug Degree have a deeper physical foundation at the cosmic scale? These questions are not meant to overturn the Big Bang Theory; they are meant to walk where it has not yet gone.

So where do we begin our exploration of cosmic evolution?

We begin from where we truly see. From observational facts that require no assumptions, no speculation—facts we can point to in the sky and say, "It is right there." We will use these nodes to construct a tree of cosmic evolution. Every leaf is a verified observational fact; every branch is a causal connection that has passed the Zero-Bug Degree test. We do not know where the crown of this tree will ultimately reach, but every footstep will land on a solid logical node.

This is not a journey with a known destination. We do not hold a complete map of the universe.

So let us begin this journey in search of the logical chain of cosmic evolution.
