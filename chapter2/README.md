# Chapter 2: The Second Law of the Universe — The Invariance of the Speed of Light

---

### 1. A Question

In Chapter 1, we established the physical basis for Zero-Bug Degree. The law of conservation of momentum proved that a system maintaining internal consistency amid change is not a philosophical fantasy, but a physical necessity.

But one of GULP's challenges remains unresolved: evolutionary dynamics. The Big Bang theory describes the process of cosmic expansion, yet it never explains where the driving force for that expansion came from. GULP must answer the same question — what drives the change in a system?

To answer this question, we need to invoke a second law.

### 2. The Principle of the Invariance of the Speed of Light

The principle of the invariance of the speed of light states: the speed of light in a vacuum is constant in all inertial reference frames, independent of the relative motion of the light source and the observer.

This constant is precisely:

c = 299,792,458 m/s

approximately three hundred thousand kilometers per second.

This principle contains two layers of meaning. First, the speed of light is a constant that does not change with the reference frame. You measure the speed of light standing on the ground, and the result is c. You measure it aboard a spaceship traveling at near light speed, and the result is still c. You race toward the light source, or you race away from it — the speed of light remains c. Second, the speed of light is independent of the motion of its source. A stationary source emits light at speed c. A source moving at near light speed still emits light at speed c. Once light is emitted, it detaches from its source and exists independently; its speed is no longer affected by the motion of the source.

This completely contradicts everyday experience. You throw a ball forward on a high-speed train, and the ball's speed relative to the ground equals the train's speed plus the speed of your throw. This is Galilean velocity addition. But if you shine a flashlight forward on that same train, the speed of light relative to the ground is not the speed of light plus the train's speed — it is still c. The law of velocity addition fails when applied to light.

### 3. The Michelson-Morley Experiment

The invariance of the speed of light was not derived from theory — it was measured by experiment.

In 1887, Michelson and Morley designed an interferometer to measure the effect of the Earth's motion through the ether on the speed of light. The physics community at the time widely believed that light propagated through a medium called "ether," just as sound propagates through air. If the Earth moved through the ether, one should be able to detect an "ether wind" — the speed of light should be faster in the direction of the ether wind and slower against it.

They used a half-silvered mirror to split a beam of light into two beams, one traveling in the direction of the Earth's motion and the other perpendicular to it. The two beams reflected back and recombined, producing interference fringes. If the speed of light differed along the two paths, the interference fringes should shift. To improve precision, they floated the entire apparatus on a pool of mercury and rotated it, taking measurements during the day and at night, across different seasons.

The result was zero.

The interference fringes never changed. No matter how they rotated the apparatus, no matter when they measured, the speed of light along both paths was exactly equal. The ether does not exist. The speed of light is invariant.

This is one of the most famous "failed experiments" in the history of physics — it failed to detect the expected result, but its null result was more important than any positive result could have been. It directly undermined the absolute space and time of Newtonian mechanics and gave birth to special relativity. And it is extraordinarily reliable — to this day, you can replicate it in any precision optics laboratory.

### 4. The Physical Meaning of the Speed of Light

The invariance of the speed of light is not an isolated law. It forces a fundamental restructuring of space and time themselves.

In Newtonian mechanics, time and space are independent and absolute. Time flows uniformly, and space remains fixed. But the invariance of the speed of light forces time and space to be linked. If the speed of light is the same in every reference frame, then time and space in different reference frames must coordinate with each other to ensure that the ratio — the speed of light — remains invariant.

This is the core of special relativity. Simultaneity is relative. Time dilates. Length contracts. These effects are not mathematical games; they have been repeatedly verified by experiment. GPS satellites must calibrate for relativistic effects, or positioning errors would accumulate by ten kilometers per day. Unstable particles in particle accelerators have their lifetimes significantly extended as they approach light speed, precisely matching relativistic predictions.

The speed of light is not merely the speed of light. It is a geometric property of spacetime itself. It is the boundary of causality.

No signal, no energy, no information can travel faster than the speed of light. This is not a technological limitation — it is a logical one. If a signal could travel faster than light, then in certain reference frames, the effect would arrive before the cause. Causality would invert. Logic would collapse.

### 5. The Invariance of the Speed of Light and the Evolutionary Curve

The invariance of the speed of light imposes a mandatory constraint on GULP's evolutionary curve.

The evolutionary curve requires that changes in a system's state maintain continuity along the time axis. The invariance of the speed of light provides a physical upper limit for this continuity: between any two moments in time, the speed at which information about a change in state can propagate cannot exceed the speed of light. If a change at point A in a system is to affect point B, that influence must propagate at a speed no greater than light.

This means the evolutionary curve is not only continuous, but causally continuous. The state at time t₁ propagates to time t₂ at a finite speed. There is no jump, no teleportation, no action at a distance.

This raises an unavoidable problem for the spatial expansion of the Big Bang theory. If the speed of spatial expansion exceeds the speed of light — and it certainly did during inflation — then causal connections between different regions of the universe are severed. An event occurring in one region cannot influence another region in any way. Such expansion cannot be driven by any known force — because the maximum speed at which any force can propagate is the speed of light. Evolutionary dynamics hits an insurmountable boundary here.

### 6. The Invariance of the Speed of Light and Zero-Bug Degree

The invariance of the speed of light also reinforces the physical foundation of Zero-Bug Degree.

Zero-Bug Degree requires a system to maintain internal consistency amid change. The invariance of the speed of light provides a time window for this requirement. Because signal propagation has an upper limit, disturbances within a system do not instantly spread throughout the whole. A local inconsistency propagates outward only at the speed of light. The system has time to react — to adjust layer by layer, to absorb disturbances locally, to resolve problems where they arise, rather than being torn apart globally in an instant.

If the speed of light were infinite — as Newtonian mechanics assumes — then any local disturbance would instantly affect the entire universe. A supernova would explode, and the entire universe would simultaneously feel the change in its gravitational field. A local inconsistency would instantly become a global inconsistency, and Zero-Bug Degree could never be maintained in such a universe.

The invariance of the speed of light gives disturbances a finite propagation time and gives systems room to adjust. It is the second safeguard of Zero-Bug Degree in the physical world.
